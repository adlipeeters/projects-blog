Our company operates within a competitive marketplace and must adhere to strict competition laws enforced by the European Commission. Failure to comply can result in significant fines, reputational damage, and even criminal prosecution. This document provides essential guidance on key areas of competition law that are most relevant to our operations.

## Price-Fixing and Market Allocation
Price-fixing and market allocation agreements are strictly prohibited under Article 101 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). These practices involve colluding with competitors to set prices or divide markets, which can lead to substantial harm to consumers and distortion of competition. For instance, in 2018, a major European food producer was fined €857 million for engaging in price-fixing schemes. To avoid such penalties, ensure that all discussions with competitors are conducted transparently and focus solely on legitimate business practices.

## Abuse of Dominant Position
Article 102 of the TFEU prohibits the abuse of a dominant position within the internal market. A company is considered dominant if it holds a market share of 50% or more in a particular market segment. Brightmoor Foods currently holds a 35% market share in the European packaged soup market, which, while significant, does not classify us as a dominant player. However, we must still be cautious not to engage in practices such as predatory pricing, exclusive dealing agreements, or tying arrangements that could be perceived as abusive. For example, offering deeply discounted prices to secure long-term contracts with major retailers could be scrutinized if it appears to aim to eliminate competitors.

## Mergers and Acquisitions
The European Commission reviews mergers and acquisitions that may significantly affect competition. If the combined market share of the merging parties exceeds certain thresholds, a Phase II investigation may be triggered. Currently, our proposed acquisition of SmallBatch Delights GmbH, a German snack manufacturer with a 12% market share in the European organic snack market, would result in a combined market share of 28%. This is below the 35% threshold that typically triggers a Phase II investigation, but we must still submit a detailed merger notification by **15 October 2024** to ensure compliance.

## Leniency Programme
The European Commission's Leniency Programme offers immunity or reduced fines for companies that report cartels to the authorities. If you suspect any anti-competitive behaviour within our organisation or among competitors, immediately report it to the Legal Department. Confidentiality is strictly maintained, and whistleblowers are protected under our company policy.

Adhering to these guidelines will help protect Brightmoor Foods from legal risks and uphold our commitment to fair competition.
