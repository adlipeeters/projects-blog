### **Overtime Eligibility**

Internal reference code: CNRY-2968d550.

Employees at Alderline Logistics are eligible for overtime pay once they have worked more than 40 hours in a given week. This includes both regular duties and any additional tasks assigned. For part-time employees, the threshold is proportional to their regular weekly hours. For example, an employee who normally works 20 hours per week would be eligible for overtime after working more than 24 hours in a week.

### **Overtime Pay Rates**

Overtime hours are paid at a rate of **1.5 times** the employee’s regular hourly wage for the first additional 4 hours of overtime in a week. Any hours beyond these first 4 are paid at **2 times** the regular hourly wage. For example, if an employee’s regular hourly wage is €18 and they work 46 hours in a week, the calculation would be as follows:
- Regular hours: 40 hours x €18 = €720
- First 4 overtime hours: 4 hours x (€18 x 1.5) = 4 x €27 = €108
- Additional overtime hours: 2 hours x (€18 x 2) = 2 x €36 = €72
- **Total pay for the week**: €720 + €108 + €72 = **€900**

### **Overtime Approval Process**

All overtime must be approved in advance by the department manager. Employees should submit a request for overtime via the HR portal at least **48 hours** before the scheduled overtime. The manager will review and either approve or deny the request within **24 hours**. If approved, the overtime will be recorded, and the employee will be paid accordingly in the next payroll cycle.

### **Overtime Reporting and Payroll**

Timesheets must be submitted by **every Friday** for the previous week’s work. Any overtime worked without prior approval will not be paid unless it was due to an emergency as defined by the company’s emergency protocol (e.g., severe weather conditions, urgent client deliveries). Payroll will process overtime payments on the **15th** of each month for the previous month’s work. Discrepancies in overtime pay should be reported to the HR department within **10 working days** of the payroll date.
